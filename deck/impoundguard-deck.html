Why it keeps happening

Nobody has one view of what lapses next.

Certificates live in a shoebox, or scattered across WhatsApp photos nobody ever filed. When money is tight and only one vehicle can be renewed this week, the owner renews whichever earns the most.

That quietly means the vehicle carrying fourteen passengers a day can lose out to the van carrying nobody — because nothing is weighing safety against revenue. Only memory against panic.
How it works

One photograph closes the loop.

STEP 01

Photograph

Any phone, any angle. Glare, shadow and a crumpled page are the normal case, not the exception.

STEP 02

Extract

One vision-model call reads the document and returns plate, holder, issue and expiry as structured fields.

STEP 03

Match

The plate is a lookup against the fleet, never a guess. Anything the model misreads is corrected before it commits.

STEP 04

Re-rank

The whole fleet reorders live — visibly, in front of the room, not on a refresh.

Strip out the vision call and the owner is back to guessing from memory. The AI is load-bearing, not decoration.
The risk engine

A ranking you can read out loud.

score = 100 × urgency × ( w·safety + (1−w)·revenue )
Urgency

How close the worst document is to lapsing, ramped over 30 days. Expired pins it to the top.

Revenue

Daily income, relative to the fleet’s highest earner.

Safety

Passengers carried daily, relative to the fleet’s fullest vehicle.

Multiplying by urgency is deliberate: a vehicle with months of cover scores near zero no matter what it earns. And w is a slider the owner controls — the ethics are on screen, not buried in the code.

Privacy by design

We keep the fields. We never keep the photograph.

Every certificate carries a holder’s name — personal data under POPIA. Confirmed certificates are written to Postgres, because reminding an operator before a document lapses means knowing their fleet when nobody has the app open. The image itself is downscaled in the browser, sent once for extraction, and discarded.

Stored, and why

Plate, holder name, document number and dates — the minimum needed to rank risk and warn ahead of an expiry. Two tables, both readable in the repo.

What a pilot would add

This build has no accounts, no access control and no retention limit. A real deployment needs all three, plus consent for holder names at signup.

Storing the fields and discarding the image is the smallest footprint that still does the job.
